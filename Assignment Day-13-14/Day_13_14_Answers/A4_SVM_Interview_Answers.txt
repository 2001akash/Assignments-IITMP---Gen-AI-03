SVM INTERVIEW QUESTIONNAIRE - ANSWERS

1. Explain SVM simply.
   SVM chooses a decision boundary that separates classes while keeping the nearest training points as far from it as possible.
2. Why maximum-margin classifier?
   It maximizes the distance from the boundary to the closest observations, which can help generalization.
3. What are support vectors?
   Training observations on or inside the margin; they determine the fitted boundary.
4. How does it find the hyperplane?
   It solves a convex optimization problem minimizing weight norm while satisfying margin constraints, with slack for overlap.
5. Hard vs soft margin?
   Hard margin requires perfect separation; soft margin allows violations and penalizes them through C.
6. Why kernels?
   Kernels let the model represent nonlinear boundaries through feature-space inner products.
7. Kernel trick example?
   An RBF kernel compares points by exp(-gamma times squared distance), enabling nonlinear separation without explicitly building infinitely many features.
8. Linear vs nonlinear SVM?
   Linear SVM uses a hyperplane in the input features; nonlinear SVM uses a kernel such as RBF to form curved boundaries.
9. When choose linear over RBF?
   For sparse high-dimensional data, large datasets, interpretability, or lower inference cost when performance is adequate.
10. What does C represent?
   C controls the trade-off between a wide margin and penalizing training violations.
11. How does C affect model?
   Small C tolerates more violations and stronger regularization; large C fits training examples more strictly and may overfit.
12. What is gamma?
   For RBF, gamma controls how local each training point?s influence is.
13. Gamma too high or low?
   Very high gamma can overfit with narrow local influence; very low gamma can underfit with overly smooth similarities.
14. Why scale features?
   SVM distances and margins depend on feature scale, especially with RBF kernels.
15. Can SVM handle multiclass?
   Yes; libraries commonly use one-versus-one or one-versus-rest strategies.
16. Advantages?
   Strong margins, effective high-dimensional separation, flexible kernels, and a convex training objective.
17. Limitations?
   Kernel training can be slow on large datasets; tuning and scaling matter; probabilities are not automatic; models can be less interpretable.
18. Why effective high-dimensional?
   The max-margin objective can generalize well when the feature space is large, including sparse text representations.
19. Noise or overlap?
   Use soft margins and tune C; assess class-specific performance and avoid fitting noisy points too aggressively.
20. Overfitting and prevention?
   Overfitting is a boundary too tailored to training data; control C/gamma, scale correctly, cross-validate, and use held-out evaluation.
21. SVM vs logistic regression?
   SVM focuses on margin and decision scores; logistic regression optimizes log loss and naturally yields probabilities, though calibration may still be needed.
22. SVM vs trees?
   SVM builds global margin boundaries; trees split features into rules and are often easier to explain but can be unstable.
23. SVM vs neural nets?
   SVM solves a convex margin problem for fixed kernel/hyperparameters; neural nets learn layered representations with nonconvex optimization.
24. What metrics?
   Accuracy plus precision, recall, F1, ROC AUC, average precision, and confusion matrices as suited to class balance and costs.
25. What is SVR?
   Support Vector Regression predicts a continuous value using an epsilon-insensitive tolerance tube.
26. SVR vs traditional regression?
   SVR penalizes errors outside an epsilon tube and can use kernels; least squares penalizes all residuals quadratically.
27. Optimization technique?
   Convex quadratic programming for standard SVM formulations.
28. Why expensive?
   Kernel methods can require many pairwise similarities and memory/time that grow steeply with sample count.
29. Real project example?
   A linear SVM can classify sparse labeled support tickets or documents when latency and compact inference matter.
30. How improve poor SVM?
   Check labels and leakage, scale in a pipeline, validate the split, inspect class imbalance, tune C/kernel/gamma, improve features, and compare operational metrics.
